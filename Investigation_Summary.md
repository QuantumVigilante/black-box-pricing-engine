# Investigation Summary

- Started with a sanity check `quote(2.0, 100, "standard")` just to make sure I could actually call the function and get a normal-looking number back before doing anything else.
- Tried varying the weight while keeping everything else fixed. At first the prices looked kind of random until I zoomed into smaller steps and realized the weight was getting rounded up to the nearest 0.5 kg before it was even billed, that's why several different weights in a row gave the exact same price.
- Did the same thing with distance, holding weight fixed. This one was more straightforward and gave me the base formula: `40 × billable_weight + 2.5 × distance_km`.
- Tested all six categories at the same weight/distance to see how they differed. `standard`, `books`, `clothing`, and `food` all gave identical prices, so those don't do anything. `electronics` came out to exactly 1.3× the standard price, and `fragile` looked like a flat +150 rather than a percentage.
- Noticed a weird price drop around 14 kg and again around 289 km distance, and my first guess was that there was a discount if weight ≥ 14 OR distance ≥ 289. That turned out to be wrong `quote(14.0, 1, "standard")` gave the normal, non-discounted price, which broke that theory. After more testing it turned out to be a single rule: if the subtotal goes over $800, you get 10% off. Weight and distance were both just ways of pushing the subtotal past 800.
- Also checked whether that $800 cutoff was checked before or after the category adjustment, since fragile/electronics change the subtotal. Turns out it's checked after, I could get the discount to kick in with `fragile` even when the plain `standard` price for the same weight/distance was under $800.
- For the coupon, I assumed "WELCOME10" meant 10% off because of the name, but that was wrong too, it's just a flat -$100 no matter how big the order is. Also tried lowercase and versions with extra spaces, and none of those worked, so it has to match exactly.
- Tested `express=True` vs `False` across every category and a bunch of order sizes, including with the coupon on. It never once changed the price, so I concluded it just doesn't do anything.
- Once I had all the rules, I wrote `my_quote.py` and checked it against the real oracle with 648 different test cases (covering the rounding boundaries, the $800 edge, all categories, both express values, and different coupons), everything matched exactly.
